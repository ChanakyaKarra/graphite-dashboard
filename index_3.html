Calibration notes
Option B — 4 accounting buckets (CN baseline shares):
• Equipment 48% — Acheson furnaces (largest single line), spheronizers, jet/ACM mills, coaters, crucibles depreciation. Tradeable globally; West premium 2.0–2.5×.
• Installation 25% — foundations, piping, instrumentation, install labor. Domestic-labor driven; West premium 5–6×.
• Indirect (engineering + EPC) 20% — design, project management, owner's cost. Strongly local; West premium 5.5–6.5×.
• Contingency 7% — project-risk reserve, typically 10% of total capex. Higher for greenfield in unproven markets.

• Sub-scale ceiling capped at 85% premium (pilot plants <1,500 tpa hit fixed-cost burdens beyond pure-formula extrapolation).

Cost model assumptions (calibrated to Bhuwalka 2025 Fig. 3)
China baseline (Acheson, calcined pet coke, uncoated):
• Total: $5,415/t (Bhuwalka 2025 Fig. 3b — "China synthetic" column).
• Capital share: 30.1% ($1,630/t). Operating share: 69.9% ($3,785/t).
• Key process inputs: petroleum coke $0.78/kg (14.4%), graphitization $2.36/kg (43.5% — electricity + packing coke + crucibles), coating $0.81/kg (14.9%), G&A $0.49/kg (9.1%).

United States baseline (Acheson, calcined pet coke, uncoated):
• Total: $12,312/t — 2.28× China (Bhuwalka 2025 Fig. 3a).
• Capital share: 50.6% ($6,228/t — 3.88× China). Operating share: 49.4%.
• Petroleum coke premium: $1.21/kg US vs $0.78/kg China (1.55×). Graphitization: 50.7% of total.

Wright's Law (volume scaling):
• Reference plant size: 25,000 tpa; learning rate: 15% per doubling (Ziegler & Trancik 2020 give 20–24% for Li-ion cells; graphite is more mature → 12–18% range; we use 15% mid).
• Floor: asymptotes to −22% below reference at very large scale (mature-industry single-line ceiling — Chinese plants >100k tpa already at this level).
• Curve range: 2,000 → 100,000 tpa; above 100k tpa requires multi-line plants.

Quality vs. cost trade-off (degree of graphitization)
d₀₀₂ interpretation (interlayer spacing, Mering-Maire equation):
• Pure single-crystal graphite: d₀₀₂ = 0.3354 nm (DoG = 100%).
• Battery-grade synthetic anode: d₀₀₂ ≤ 0.3370 nm required (DoG ≥ 90%).
• Industrial graphite (electrodes, refractories): d₀₀₂ ≤ 0.339 nm acceptable (DoG ≥ 70%).

Process trade-off: Higher peak temperature and longer hold time push d₀₀₂ closer to 0.3354 (better quality) but consume more energy. EFB is the most energy-efficient (89% avg) and recent counterflow designs reach 2,700°C (AETC ACS Omega 2025; BNL pilot) — bringing DoG into battery-grade range (86–96%). LWG and VTF still achieve the highest DoG (95–99%) at moderate energy cost.

Sources: Emmerich 1995 (Carbon 33, 1709–1715); Ragan & Marsh 1983 (J. Mater. Sci. 18, 3161–3176); Wang et al. ACS 2025 (Tsinghua/Aramco, Acheson vs continuous comparison); AETC ACS Omega 2025 (doi:10.1021/acsomega.5c05566 — EFB peak 2,700°C validation).

How the two papers reconcile — SEC anchor derivation
Sharma NRM 2026 gives direct-stated total plant SEC: "Synthetic and natural graphite incur high on-site electricity usage (36 kWh kg⁻¹ and 8.7 kWh kg⁻¹) because of energy-intensive electrified thermal processes such as graphitization (3,000°C) and coating (1,300°C)." This is the whole synthetic plant on-site electricity: graphitization + coating + shaping + granulation + milling + utilities = 36,000 kWh/t.

Steer 2025 gives an implicit location-sensitive SEC via Mongolia: "Electricity prices ... lower in inner Mongolia — where most graphitization in China occurs — than in the southeast U.S. (difference in costs of $240/t)." At the paper's 6.5¢–4¢ = 2.5¢/kWh price gap, $240/t ÷ $0.025 = 9,600 kWh/t. This is the graphitization-step-only electricity that can be moved by relocation.

Reconciliation: Sharma's 36,000 kWh/t includes coating (1,300°C — locked at plant), utilities, motors, drying, and quality control — all of which use nationally-averaged electricity. Steer's 9,600 kWh/t is the portion that can be sourced from cheap regional electricity (i.e., graphitization). Dashboard Acheson SEC = 15,000 kWh/t reflects graphitization step alone (~42% of Sharma's 36,000 total), midpoint of Steer 9,600 and full electrode-heating literature 20,000 kWh/t. Other furnace SECs scaled by relative efficiency vs Acheson (Prominer 2025; Wang ACS 2025).

Validation notes & structural insights
Anode-grade availability & pricing dynamics:
• Aluminum/anode crossover: Sponge coke (low-S) is 93% of CPC output — battery anodes compete with aluminum smelters for the same anode-grade feed. Q1 2026 sees structural tightness as Chinese sulfur limits below 3% restrict effective feedstock pool (Mordor 2026).
• Needle coke premium: 1.85–1.96× CPC — driven by EAF steel + battery anode dual demand. Phillips 66 + Epsilon Advanced Materials Sep-2025 supply deal for 30k tpa NC plant (delayed to 2027, Argus) shows long-term contracts displacing spot.
• Green pet coke gap: Anode-grade GPC ($650–800/t) is sharply distinguished from fuel-grade GPC ($390–590/t). Only ~25% of GPC output meets anode spec (Marlin Energy). Model uses 0.55× to reflect the purification-burden midpoint between fuel grade and CPC.
• CTP / ETP as binders: Coal tar pitch and ethylene tar pitch are also used as the granulation-step binder. EU REACH cap on 18 PAHs at 50 mg/kg (effective Apr-2026) drives 15–25% premium for ultra-low-PAH grades (Mordor 2026).
• Recycled graphite: Skips graphitization (>75% of synth cost per Bhuwalka 2025). DOE pilots reach 99.8–99.9% purity but commercial scale is post-2028 (Mordor). Sharma et al. 2026 (Nat. Rev. Mater.) projects 5–7% of demand by 2027.

🇨🇳 China Import Duty — context-aware
Applies only when the importing market (L1 or L2) is non-China. Raises China's effective landed price as seen by L1's and L2's domestic markets — a "tariff wall" against Chinese-origin graphite. If L1 or L2 is China, no duty applies (China is the source, not the importer). Effect: shifts the China parity line upward in the Competitive Volume Curve and Cost Comparison table. Update (Mar 2026): Commerce's Feb-2026 final AD/CVD on Chinese active anode material (AD ~93.5%, CVD ~66.7%, ~160% combined) were NOT imposed — the USITC returned a negative injury vote on 12 Mar 2026, terminating the order. Section 301 (+25%) remains the in-force US duty; the AD/CVD presets below are retained as hypothetical scenarios only.
